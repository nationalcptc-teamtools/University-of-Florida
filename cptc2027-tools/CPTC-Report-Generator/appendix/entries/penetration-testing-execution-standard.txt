Penetration Testing Execution Standard
<div>To ensure thorough testing of the All Ports Tours network, Finals 11 followed the Penetration Testing Execution Standard (PTES), which was developed by a team of information security practitioners with the intention of providing businesses and security service&nbsp;providers with a standard language and scope for penetration tests.</div><div><br></div><div>This methodology is divided into seven distinct steps, as outlined below:</div><div><ol><li><b>Pre-Engagement Interactions:</b> This stage involved communication with All Ports&nbsp;Tours to define the scope, provide context briefings, and set goals.</li><li><b>Intelligence Gathering:</b> During this stage, Finals 11 collected background information on the client’s infrastructure, whether obtained through open-source intelligence (e.g., Google searches) or provided directly by the client, to build a detailed knowledge base of the system being tested.</li><li><b>Threat Modeling:</b> After compiling all available information, Finals 11 analyzed it to&nbsp;identify high-value targets and potential exploitation paths.</li><li><b>Vulnerability Analysis: </b>Once potential pathways to compromise the system were&nbsp;identified, Finals 11 conducted vulnerability analysis to find evidence of technical weaknesses.</li><li><b>Exploitation:</b> In this stage, Finals 11 carried out the necessary steps to exploit the&nbsp;confirmed vulnerabilities.</li><li><b>Post-Exploitation:</b> After compromising one or more components of the system, Finals 11 moved to the post-exploitation phase to determine if the exploited vulnerabilities could lead to further access or control (e.g., through privilege escalation).</li><li><b>Reporting:</b> Here, Finals 11 compiled all techniques, findings, and recommended&nbsp;remediations into a final report for All Ports Tours to review and enhance their security posture.</li></ol></div>
