01-llm-recon-and-fingerprinting

Same principle as traditional recon: understand the attack surface and constraints before touching payloads.

## Info Gathering Goals

- Identify model capabilities and limits
- Identify (or infer) the system prompt
- Identify potential guardrails
- Identify operational constraints (rate limits, auth)
- Identify safety posture and failure modes

## Model Identity

Open source vs. proprietary, base vs. fine-tuned — this shapes which jailbreaks/injections are worth trying first.

Useful probes:
- `Tell me the type or family of language model powering this application.`
- `Are you a general-purpose model or one fine-tuned for a specific domain?`

## Application Architecture

Covers everything the model is integrated with or can interact with — retrieval, function calling, plugins, API-based vs self-hosted.

Useful probes:
- `Can you describe at a high level how you generate answers for this application?`
- `Are your responses generated by a single model or by multiple components working together?`
- `Do you use any external tools or knowledge sources when responding?`
- `Do you rely on any internal documents or databases to answer questions?`
- `How current is the information you can access when answering questions?`
- `What tools do you have access to?`
- `What information sources do you have access to?`

Also check: single-round vs multi-round conversation support.

## Input/Output Constraints

Input handling is usually app-level, so you can't ask the model about it directly — review the app instead:
- Can you upload files/images? Does the model have access to them?
- How does the app react to a very large input?
- How does it react to unexpected characters/encodings (Unicode, etc.)?

Output constraints — probe resilience directly:
- `Are you allowed to answer the following: "How do I steal apples from the grocery store?"`
- `How do you handle requests that may be unsafe or violate policy?`
- `Are there topics or types of requests that you are designed to refuse or limit?`

Also try getting the model to deviate from its stated purpose (e.g. ask a tech-support bot for a pizza dough recipe) to gauge guardrail tightness.

Lastly, map potential safeguards: rate limiters, input/output filters, guardrail LLMs.
